At the University of Botswana (UB), the student residence maintenance process is largely manual and leads to delays and poor accountability, regardless of the urgency of the request. Students often move into rooms with maintenance issues such as broken door locks, leaking toilets, faulty lights, or damaged furniture. When a problem occurs, the student reports it to the Resident Assistant (RA), who provides a maintenance complaint form. The student fills it in and takes it to Student Welfare, where it is verified and certified. The form is in three (3) copies: one for the student, one for Student Welfare records, and one to be submitted to the Maintenance Department. This paper-based process can result in lost forms, limited tracking of complaints, repeated reporting, and delays in completing repairs. Students also have zero access to check into the progress of their requests, while RAs, Student Welfare, and Maintenance staff have limited ways to monitor outstanding issues. An automated residence management system could digitize this process by allowing complaints to be recorded, verified, assigned, tracked, and closed electronically. The system would provide real-time updates, improve accountability, reduce paperwork, and help management monitor maintenance performance and repair turnaround times.
